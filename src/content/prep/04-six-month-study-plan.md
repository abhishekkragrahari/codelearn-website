Six months is enough to cover the full GATE CS syllabus once, revise it twice and take regular mock tests, if you study steadily. This plan assumes about **4–5 hours a day** on weekdays and **6–8 hours** on weekends, alongside college or a job.

## How the plan is built

- **Foundations first.** Discrete Mathematics, C programming and Data Structures are used by almost every other subject, so they come first.
- **Pair a heavy subject with a light one** each week, so you never spend a full week on only one difficult topic.
- **Every Sunday:** solve the week's practice set and revise your notes from the previous weeks.
- **One subject done = one timed subject test.** Each track's GATE practice set works as this test.

## Month-by-month plan

| Weeks | Main subject | Side subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | [C Programming](../c/index.html) (pointers, recursion, storage classes) | [General Aptitude](../ga/index.html): numbers, percentages |
| 3–4 | [Data Structures](../dsa/index.html): arrays to heaps | [Discrete Mathematics](../dm/index.html): logic, sets, relations |
| 5–6 | [Algorithms](../dsa/complexity.html): complexity, sorting, graphs, greedy, DP | Discrete Mathematics: combinatorics, recurrences, graph theory |
| 7–8 | [Digital Logic](../dl/index.html) | [Engineering Mathematics](../em/index.html): linear algebra |
| 9–10 | [Computer Organization](../coa/index.html) | Engineering Mathematics: calculus, probability |
| 11–12 | [Operating Systems](../os/index.html) | General Aptitude: ratio, work, speed |
| 13–14 | [Databases](../dbms/index.html) | General Aptitude: data interpretation, reasoning |
| 15–16 | [Computer Networks](../cn/index.html) | Revise C and DSA |
| 17–18 | [Theory of Computation](../toc/index.html) | Revise Digital Logic and COA |
| 19 | [Compiler Design](../cd/index.html) | General Aptitude: verbal ability |
| 20–22 | **Revision 1:** all subjects, using the [formula sheet](formula-sheet.html) | One previous year paper per week |
| 23–24 | **Revision 2 + mocks:** two full mocks per week, analyse every mistake | Re-solve wrong questions |
| 25–26 | **Final phase:** light revision, formula sheet, rest before the exam | One mock per week |

The plan covers 26 weeks, which is about six months.

## A typical study day

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 2 hours | New theory from the main subject: read the lesson, make short notes |
| 1 hour | Practice questions on what you read today |
| 1 hour | Side subject |
| 30 minutes | Revise yesterday's notes and formulas |

## Rules that make the plan work

1. **Short notes, not long notes.** One page per lesson: definitions, formulas and the traps you fell into.
2. **Spaced revision.** Revise a topic after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. A topic you read once is mostly forgotten by exam day.
3. **Solve, don't just read.** For every hour of theory, do at least 30 minutes of questions.
4. **Track your mistakes** in a notebook, by subject. Before every mock, read this notebook.
5. **Don't skip General Aptitude.** It is 15 marks of the easiest questions in the paper.

## If you fall behind

Do not drop a subject entirely. Cut the side-subject slot for a week, and protect the revision weeks at the end: revision gives more marks per hour than first-time reading. If you have less time left, switch to the [3-month plan](three-month-study-plan.html).

## Practice

```quiz
q: The plan uses 4.5 hours each on 5 weekdays and 7 hours each on 2 weekend days. How many hours is that per week?
answer: 36.5
verify: 5 * 4.5 + 2 * 7
explain: 5 × 4.5 + 2 × 7 = 22.5 + 14 = 36.5 hours a week.
```

```quiz
q: At 36.5 hours a week, how many hours does the full 26-week plan give you?
answer: 949
verify: 26 * 36.5
explain: 26 × 36.5 = 949 hours.
```

