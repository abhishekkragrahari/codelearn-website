Three months is short for the whole GATE CS syllabus, so this plan is about **priorities**: cover the high-yield subjects well, do the rest at a working level, and practise a lot. It assumes about **7–8 hours a day**. If you have already studied the subjects once, use it as a revision plan.

## Priorities

Spend most time on subjects that are large in the syllabus and quick to score:

- **First:** Algorithms and Data Structures, Operating Systems, Computer Networks, Databases, Discrete and Engineering Mathematics, General Aptitude.
- **Next:** Theory of Computation, Computer Organization, Digital Logic.
- **Then:** Compiler Design, C programming output questions.

No subject is skipped: even the smaller ones give a few marks that are often easy.

## Daily targets

- Finish one lesson's theory and **at least 20 questions** on it every day.
- End each day by writing the formulas and traps of the day on one page.
- Every 7th day: no new theory. Revise the week and solve that week's [practice sets](previous-year-papers.html).

## Mock test routine

1. Take the mock in one sitting of 3 hours, using only an on-screen-style calculator.
2. Mark each wrong or skipped question as *concept gap*, *calculation slip* or *time*.
3. Fix concept gaps the next morning; for slips, re-solve slowly; for time, practise more short questions.

## Last-week checklist

- Admit card printed and the exam centre visited or located on a map.
- Valid photo ID as specified in the brochure.
- Formula sheet and mistake notebook read twice.
- No new topics in the last three days. Sleep well the night before.

## Practice

```quiz
q: With 7.5 hours a day for 12 weeks and one rest day each week, how many study hours do you get?
answer: 540
verify: 12 * 6 * 7.5
explain: 12 weeks × 6 study days × 7.5 hours = 540 hours.
```

```quiz
q: A student scores 52, 58 and 61 in three mocks. What is the average mock score? (Give 2 decimal places.)
answer: 57.00
verify: round((52 + 58 + 61) / 3, 2)
explain: (52 + 58 + 61) / 3 = 171 / 3 = 57.
```

```quiz
q: In mock analysis, a question you knew how to solve but got wrong because of an arithmetic error is a:
- Concept gap
* Calculation slip
- Time problem
- Syllabus gap
explain: You knew the method but made an arithmetic mistake: a calculation slip. Fix it by re-solving slowly and checking with the virtual calculator.
```
