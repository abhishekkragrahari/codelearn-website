**Joining** makes one part from several. Welding melts (or presses) the parts together; brazing and soldering use a filler that melts at a lower temperature than the parts.

## Arc welding

An electric arc (about 6000 °C) melts the base and filler metals.

- **Arc power** P = V I; **heat input per unit length** = η V I / v (η = heat transfer efficiency, v = travel speed).
- **Arc length–voltage characteristic:** V = A + B l (voltage grows with arc length). With a drooping power source, the operating point is where the arc characteristic meets the source line.
- Processes: **SMAW** (stick, flux-coated electrode), **GMAW/MIG** (consumable wire + inert gas), **GTAW/TIG** (non-consumable tungsten electrode), **SAW** (submerged arc, thick plates), plasma arc.
- **Polarity:** DCEN (electrode negative) puts more heat into the work, giving deep penetration; DCEP gives more heat at the electrode.

## Gas welding

Oxy-acetylene flame: **neutral** (equal O₂ and C₂H₂, most metals), **oxidising** (excess O₂, brass), **carburising** (excess acetylene, high-carbon steels). Hottest point ≈ 3200 °C at the tip of the inner cone.

## Resistance welding

Heat is generated by the resistance of the joint:

**H = I² R t**

Spot, seam, projection and flash butt welding. Very fast and used for sheet metal in car bodies.

## Solid-state welding

No melting: friction welding, **friction stir welding** (aluminium), ultrasonic, explosive and diffusion welding.

## Heat-affected zone and defects

- The **HAZ** is base metal heated enough to change its structure but not melted. In steels it can become hard and brittle.
- Defects: porosity, slag inclusion, lack of fusion, incomplete penetration, undercut, and cracks (hot cracks while solidifying; cold cracks from hydrogen).

## Brazing, soldering and adhesives

- **Brazing:** filler melts above 450 °C, below the base metal's melting point; joined by capillary action.
- **Soldering:** filler melts below 450 °C (electronic joints).
- **Adhesive bonding:** joins dissimilar materials without heat.

## Worked example

An arc runs at 25 V and 200 A with heat transfer efficiency 0.8 and travel speed 5 mm/s.

- Arc power = 5000 W. Heat input = 0.8 × 5000/5 = **800 J/mm**.

## Practice

```quiz
q: A spot weld uses 10,000 A for 0.1 s through a joint resistance of 100 µΩ. How much heat (J) is generated?
answer: 1000
verify: round(10000 ** 2 * 100e-6 * 0.1, 6)
explain: H = I²Rt = 10⁸ × 10⁻⁴ × 0.1 = 1000 J.
```

```quiz
q: An arc welds at 20 V, 150 A, efficiency 0.75 and speed 4 mm/s. What is the heat input in J/mm?
answer: 562.5
verify: 0.75 * 20 * 150 / 4
explain: Heat input = ηVI/v = 0.75 × 3000/4 = 562.5 J/mm.
```

```quiz
q: Which welding process uses a non-consumable electrode?
- MIG (GMAW)
* TIG (GTAW)
- Submerged arc welding
- Stick (SMAW)
explain: TIG uses a tungsten electrode that does not melt; filler is added separately if needed.
```

```quiz
q: The arc characteristic is V = 20 + 4l (l in mm). What arc voltage results at an arc length of 3 mm?
answer: 32
verify: 20 + 4 * 3
explain: V = 20 + 4 × 3 = 32 V.
```

```quiz
q: An arc welds at 30 V and 250 A. What is the arc power in kW?
answer: 7.5
verify: 30 * 250 / 1000
explain: P = VI = 7500 W = 7.5 kW.
```

```quiz
q: In oxy-acetylene welding, which flame is used for most steels?
* Neutral flame
- Oxidising flame
- Carburising flame
- Reducing flame with excess oxygen
explain: A neutral flame (equal oxygen and acetylene) does not change the metal's composition.
```
