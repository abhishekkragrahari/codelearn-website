The verbal half of General Aptitude tests English usage and careful reading. The questions are short, and most mistakes come from rushing, so read every option against the whole sentence.

## Subject–verb agreement

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| The verb agrees with the subject, not with a phrase in between | The list of names **is** on the desk. |
| *Each*, *every*, *either*, *neither*, *everyone* take a singular verb | Each of the students **has** submitted the form. |
| With *either … or* / *neither … nor*, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | Neither the manager nor the employees **are** attending. |
| *A number of* (many) is plural; *the number of* is singular | A number of trains **are** late. The number of trains **is** small. |
| Collective nouns are usually singular when acting as one unit | The committee **has** decided. |

## Tenses

- **Past perfect** for the earlier of two past actions: By the time we reached the station, the train **had left**.
- **Present perfect** for a past action with a present result, without a specific past time: She **has finished** the report (not "has finished it yesterday").
- After *if* in a condition about the future, use the present: If it **rains** tomorrow, the match will be postponed.
- Unreal conditions: If I **were** you, I would accept the offer.

## Articles and prepositions

- *An* before a vowel **sound**, *a* before a consonant sound: **an** honest person, **an** hour, **a** university, **a** one-day match.
- *The* for something specific or unique: **the** sun, **the** book you lent me.
- Fixed prepositions must be learnt: good **at** mathematics, interested **in**, depend **on**, consist **of**, comply **with**, prefer tea **to** coffee, different **from**.

## Pronouns

- After a preposition, use the object form: between you and **me** (not "between you and I").
- In comparisons, complete the clause to check: She is taller than **I** (am).
- *Who* is a subject, *whom* an object: the person **who** called; the person **whom** we called.

## Vocabulary in context

Questions often ask for the word closest in meaning as used **in the sentence**, so read the whole sentence:

- "The new policy was meant to **ameliorate** the housing shortage." → *improve*.
- "His **laconic** reply left us guessing." → *brief, using few words*.
- "The evidence was **corroborated** by two witnesses." → *confirmed*.

## Sentence completion

Look for signal words that show whether the blank continues or contrasts with the rest of the sentence: *although*, *despite*, *but*, *however* signal a contrast; *and*, *because*, *so*, *moreover* signal continuation.

"Despite the heavy rain, the match was not ______." The contrast word *despite* means the expected result of rain (cancellation) did not happen: **cancelled**.

## Analogies

Identify the exact relationship in the given pair, then find the option with the **same** relationship in the same order:

- Painter : brush :: writer : **pen** (a worker and their tool).
- Fish : school :: wolf : **pack** (an animal and its group).
- Thermometer : temperature :: barometer : **pressure** (an instrument and what it measures).

## Reading comprehension

Answer only from the passage. An **inference** must follow from what the passage says, without outside knowledge; an option that is true in the real world but not supported by the passage is wrong.

> **Passage.** Small towns that depend on a single crop are especially exposed to the weather. When the monsoon fails, incomes fall at the same time for nearly every household, so local shops, transporters and lenders suffer together. Towns with a mix of farming, small industry and services recover faster after a bad season, because some sources of income keep flowing even when the harvest is poor. For this reason, economists who study rural development increasingly recommend diversification over subsidies tied to one crop.

## Practice questions

```quiz
q: Choose the correct option: "Neither the coach nor the players ______ happy with the result."
- was
* were
- has been
- is
explain: With neither … nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "the players", which is plural: were.
```

```quiz
q: Choose the correctly written sentence.
- Each of the boys have finished their work.
* Each of the boys has finished his work.
- Each of the boys have finished his work.
- Each of the boys are finished their work.
explain: "Each" is singular, so it takes "has" and a singular pronoun. The phrase "of the boys" does not change the subject.
```

```quiz
q: Which word is closest in meaning to **laconic** in "His laconic reply left us guessing"?
- Angry
* Brief
- Detailed
- Cheerful
explain: A laconic reply uses very few words, which is why it left the listeners guessing.
```

```quiz
q: Complete the analogy. Thermometer : temperature :: barometer : ______
- Weather
* Pressure
- Altitude
- Rainfall
explain: A thermometer measures temperature; a barometer measures atmospheric pressure. Weather and rainfall are related to pressure but are not what the instrument measures.
```

```quiz
q: According to the passage, why do towns with a mix of activities recover faster after a bad season?
- They receive larger government subsidies
- Their farmers grow crops that need less rain
* Some of their income sources continue even when the harvest is poor
- Their shops and lenders are not affected by the monsoon
explain: The passage says such towns recover faster "because some sources of income keep flowing even when the harvest is poor". It does not mention subsidies for them or drought-resistant crops.
```

```quiz
q: Which statement can be **inferred** from the passage?
* The economists mentioned regard diversification as better protection against weather shocks than subsidies tied to one crop
- Monsoon failures have become more frequent in recent years
- Small industries are more profitable than farming
- Lenders in single-crop towns charge higher interest rates
explain: The last sentence says economists recommend diversification over crop-specific subsidies "for this reason", that is, because mixed economies keep some income flowing in a bad season. The other statements may or may not be true, but nothing in the passage supports them.
```
